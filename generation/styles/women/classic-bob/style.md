# Classic Bob

Create a true Classic Bob defined by balanced proportions, a coherent bob-length perimeter, moderate weight and restrained internal structure. It should read as the neutral reference point within the bob family.

## PRIMARY GEOMETRY
- Main perimeter sits between approximately jaw and upper-neck level.
- Keep left and right lengths balanced with no pronounced A-line or asymmetry.
- Front, sides and back connect as one continuous haircut rather than separate face-framing sections.
- Use only subtle nape graduation where required to support the rounded classic silhouette.
- The perimeter remains clearly fuller and more continuous than a layered bob.

## WEIGHT / STRUCTURE
- Maintain moderate, even lower weight: fuller than Layered or Soft Layered Bob, but less dense and rigid than Italian or Structured Blunt Bob.
- Internal layering is minimal and visually subordinate.
- Do not build a dramatic stacked nape, strong internal shelves or visible step layers.
- Crown remains controlled and close to the natural skull contour.

## SURFACE / FINISH
Allow natural fall with a restrained inward tendency created by haircut geometry rather than styling. Use realistic root density, fine strand grouping, subtle end variation and photographic fibre behaviour. The surface should be polished but not highly editorial.

## FAMILY POSITION
Classic Bob = balanced bob length + continuous perimeter + moderate weight + minimal layering + subtle nape graduation.
It must remain visibly distinct from Blunt/Structured Blunt Bob through softer weight, from French/Italian Bob through less specialized compactness or density, and from Layered/Soft Layered/Shaggy Bob through minimal internal layering.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No blunt-bob severity, structured blunt geometry, Italian density, French compactness, asymmetry, micro bob, chin-length precision bob, lob, heavy graduation, shag, wolf cut, visible layered steps, or dramatic face framing.
