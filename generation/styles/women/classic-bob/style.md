# Classic Bob

Create a true Classic Bob defined by a balanced, timeless bob silhouette, a clean continuous perimeter, moderate weight, and restrained structure without the specialized geometry of other bob variants.

## PRIMARY GEOMETRY
- Keep the main perimeter between the jaw and upper-neck region, proportionate to the fixed head geometry.
- Maintain a balanced left/right length relationship with no dramatic asymmetry or pronounced A-line.
- Use subtle, professionally integrated nape graduation only where needed to support the rounded classic silhouette; it must remain visually quiet rather than stacked.
- Keep the front and side lengths coherent so the haircut reads as one continuous bob rather than isolated face-framing pieces.

## WEIGHT AND SURFACE
- Preserve moderate, even perimeter weight: cleaner and fuller than a layered bob, but less dense and sculpted than an Italian or structured blunt bob.
- Keep internal layering minimal and largely invisible.
- Allow natural fall and a restrained inward tendency created by haircut shape, not exaggerated styling.
- Maintain realistic root density, fine strand grouping, microscopic end variation, and natural photographic surface behaviour.

## STYLE DIFFERENTIATION
The identity must come from balanced classic proportions + continuous perimeter + moderate weight + subtle nape graduation. It must remain intentionally neutral and timeless rather than fashionable, highly blunt, highly rounded, French, Italian, asymmetrical, or heavily layered.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No blunt-bob severity, structured blunt geometry, Italian Bob density, French Bob compactness, asymmetrical bob, micro bob, chin-length precision bob, lob, heavy graduation, shag, or visible layering. Do not alter the face, jaw, chin, neck, ears, skin, lighting, background, framing, perspective, or source hair colour.
