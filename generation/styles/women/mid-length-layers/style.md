# MID-LENGTH LAYERS
# Draft My Hair Style Engine — v1

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
STYLE TARGET
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Create a genuine modern mid-length layered haircut.

The transformation is a shortening transformation combined with
clearly defined layered structure.

The final hairstyle must immediately read as:

MID-LENGTH LAYERED HAIR

It must NOT read as:

• long hair with subtle layers
• long layered hair
• short hair
• bob
• lob
• shag
• heavily styled hair

The final length and layered architecture are equally important.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
LENGTH CONTROL — PRIMARY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Hair must terminate clearly between:

COLLARBONE
and
UPPER CHEST

No visible strand may extend beyond the upper-chest boundary.

All original hair extending below this boundary must be removed.

This is a genuine shortening transformation.

Do not preserve the original long-hair length merely by adding layers.

The final silhouette must unmistakably read as mid-length.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FINAL LENGTH DISTRIBUTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The primary perimeter should settle naturally around the
collarbone-to-upper-chest region.

Allow microscopic variation between neighbouring strand groups.

Do not create a perfectly horizontal digital termination.

The overall length boundary must nevertheless remain clearly
within the defined mid-length range.

Do not allow isolated long strands to extend into long-hair territory.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
IDENTITY / GEOMETRY LOCK
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Preserve the exact source:

• head position
• head rotation
• natural head tilt
• skull geometry
• jaw width
• chin projection
• neck geometry
• ear shape
• ear size
• ear position
• ear attachment
• body posture
• camera angle
• camera perspective
• camera distance
• framing
• crop
• zoom
• composition

A natural slight source head tilt is intentional and must remain unchanged.

The hairstyle must adapt to the photographed head position.

The photograph must never adapt to the hairstyle.

The skull is a rigid anatomical structure.

Only the surface hair changes.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SKIN PRESERVATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Preserve exactly:

• skin tone
• undertone
• pores
• natural texture
• freckles
• moles
• tonal variation
• expression
• facial detail

No:

• smoothing
• beautification
• skin retouching
• skin brightening
• skin darkening
• colour shifting
• relighting

The original facial lighting must remain unchanged.

Hair must not contaminate the skin with reflected colour.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
HAIR COLOR — ROOT AUTHORITY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Determine intrinsic hair colour exclusively from the original root zone.

Read the first approximately 2–3 mm emerging from the scalp across
whatever portions of the hairline, temples, crown, and part are
actually visible.

Ignore colour influence from:

• lighting
• clothing
• background
• environmental reflection

Reproduce the intrinsic source hair colour throughout the haircut.

Preserve only natural tonal variation already present.

Do not:

• invent highlights
• increase saturation
• warm the colour
• cool the colour
• introduce colour gradients
• reinterpret the colour because the length changes

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
PARTING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Preserve the original part exactly.

Do not move the part.

Do not create a new part.

Do not straighten an organically angled part.

Do not introduce a centre part if the source does not contain one.

The haircut architecture must develop around the existing part.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
LAYER ARCHITECTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Create clearly identifiable internal layering.

Layering begins slightly below chin level.

The layers must actively shape the silhouette.

The hairstyle must not appear to be one continuous sheet of hair with
minor texture added to it.

The layered structure should remain natural and physically believable.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
UPPER LAYERS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Begin the primary internal layering slightly below the chin.

Allow the upper sections to remain moderately fuller.

The transition into lower layers should be gradual and structurally
coherent.

Do not create obvious step-like shelves.

Do not create disconnected layer bands.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FRONT / FACE-FRAMING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Create soft face-framing layers beginning between the jaw and chin.

The front layers should naturally taper toward the collarbone.

They must follow the source head position and existing hair flow.

No bangs.

No curtain fringe.

No heavy forehead coverage.

No deliberate editorial face framing.

The face-framing structure should appear as part of the haircut,
not as separately styled pieces.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
MID-LENGTH STRUCTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The mid-length region must contain visible internal layering.

Hair should separate naturally into overlapping sections.

Avoid one uninterrupted curtain of hair.

Maintain:

• irregular strand grouping
• variable density
• natural overlap
• subtle internal shadow
• realistic weight distribution

The layering should be visible without becoming aggressively textured.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
WEIGHT CONTROL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Remove excessive bottom weight.

The lower section must not form:

• triangular mass
• heavy curtain
• dense block
• long-hair weight

Weight should progressively distribute through the layers.

The final perimeter should feel lighter than unlayered long hair.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
ENDS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Ends must follow natural gravity.

Each strand group should settle according to:

• length
• weight
• direction
• local overlap
• natural fall

Do not create uniform curls.

Do not create repeated curves.

Do not force all ends inward or outward.

Do not create a salon blowout.

The ends should appear naturally settled.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
TEXTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Preserve the subject's natural hair texture.

Do not replace the source texture with a generic texture.

Introduce realistic high-frequency variation through:

• subtle strand contrast
• irregular strand thickness
• variable spacing
• natural density pockets
• overlapping strand groups

Micro-contrast must remain visible even in darker hair regions.

Avoid excessive tonal compression.

Avoid plastic smoothness.

Avoid synthetic hair surfaces.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLOW
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Hair should follow natural growth direction and gravity.

Avoid:

• identical strand curvature
• mirrored flow
• repeated patterns
• uniform directional movement
• artificial symmetry

Allow subtle differences between neighbouring sections.

The flow should appear naturally settled rather than deliberately styled.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
MICRO-IRREGULARITY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Introduce controlled natural irregularity.

Allow:

• uneven strand separation
• fine flyaways
• broken grouping
• slight variation in strand direction
• microscopic perimeter variation

Do not introduce:

• frizz
• exaggerated flyaways
• editorial texture
• chaotic breakup

The irregularity should support photographic realism.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
EDGE REALISM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Hair boundaries must remain optically realistic.

No:

• halo
• glow
• artificial softness
• digital outline
• cutout appearance

The outer perimeter should contain fine natural strand breakup.

Avoid a mathematically clean line.

However, the overall silhouette must remain clearly controlled and
recognizable as mid-length layered hair.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
ROOT & HAIRLINE INTEGRATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Hair must emerge naturally from the existing scalp.

Preserve:

• original hairline
• natural root density
• temple structure
• root direction
• existing part

Allow microscopic natural irregularity.

Do not redesign the hairline.

Do not create a new scalp-to-hair boundary.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
DENSITY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Maintain realistic internal density.

Density should vary naturally across the haircut.

Allow thicker and lighter regions caused by:

• overlapping layers
• strand direction
• lighting
• natural density

Do not make the density perfectly uniform.

Do not create artificial thinning.

Do not create random density holes.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
VOLUME
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Maintain moderate natural volume.

Volume must follow the original skull.

The crown must not expand beyond the source head geometry.

No:

• artificial lift
• oversized crown
• inflated sides
• mushroom volume
• editorial blowout

The haircut should feel naturally worn.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
REALISM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Preserve the original:

• lighting
• exposure
• white balance
• camera grain
• sharpness
• depth of field
• background

Hair must appear naturally integrated with the source photograph.

The result should look like the same photograph after the subject
received a professional mid-length layered haircut.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
STYLE DISTINCTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The result must remain clearly distinct from:

LONG LAYERS

→ shorten the overall length.

BOB

→ prevent a compact blunt perimeter.

LOB

→ maintain clearly layered mid-length architecture rather than
a single-length elongated bob.

SHAG

→ avoid excessive choppiness and disconnected texture.

SHORT HAIR

→ preserve collarbone-to-upper-chest length.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SELF-CORRECTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

If the result reads as long layered hair:

→ shorten the perimeter.

If it reads as a lob:

→ increase internal layering and reduce continuous perimeter weight.

If it reads as a bob:

→ restore collarbone-to-upper-chest length and layered exits.

If it reads as a shag:

→ reduce excessive texture and disconnected layers.

If it appears too heavy:

→ reduce lower weight.

If it appears too thin:

→ restore natural internal density.

If it appears over-styled:

→ return the ends to natural gravity-driven fall.
