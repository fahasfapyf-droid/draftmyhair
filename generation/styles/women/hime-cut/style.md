# Hime Cut

## 1. INPAINT HAIR ONLY

Modify only the hair. The face, facial features, expression, eyes, brows, skin texture, skin tone, jawline, chin, neck, ears, body, clothing, background, lighting, exposure, color balance, focus, grain, pose, framing, perspective and source scale remain completely untouched.

## 2. GEOMETRY LOCK — ABSOLUTE

Preserve the exact head position, tilt and viewing angle. Any natural head tilt in the source is intentional and must remain unchanged. Jaw shape and width are locked. Chin is locked. Neck is locked. Ear shape locked — do not regenerate the ears. The skull is a rigid fixed object. Only the surface hair changes. Match the source scale and framing exactly; no zoom, crop or reframe. Do not reshape the skull or alter the position of the ears.

## 3. SKIN PRESERVATION — CRITICAL

Preserve the exact facial and visible skin texture, pores, tone and natural micro-variation. No smoothing, beauty filter, retouching, relighting or skin-tone drift. Hair must not cast artificial color onto the face or scalp. Never lighten the source skin tone.

## 4. HAIR COLOR — ROOT AUTHORITY

STEP 1 — Read true hair color ONLY from the first 2–3 mm at the scalp, sampling the hairline, temples, part and nape where visible, and use that root-zone evidence as the authority.

STEP 2 — Ignore and discard color information coming from the background, lighting, environment, clothing or reflected surroundings.

STEP 3 — Reproduce the exact source root color and only the natural color variation already present in the source hair. Do not invent highlights, lowlights, dye or artificial color shift.

STEP 4 — White-background contamination check: would the reproduced hair color still look correct on a pure white background? If no, correct the contamination before completing the result.

## 5. STYLE-SPECIFIC DESIGN

Create a true Hime Cut defined by three unmistakable structural zones: long straight lower lengths, short blunt side locks beside the face, and a distinct straight fringe when a fringe is part of the intended Hime structure.

### CORE GEOMETRY
- Preserve a long overall length as the dominant lower foundation.
- Create two substantial, blunt side-lock sections at approximately cheekbone-to-jaw level, ending clearly and horizontally/cleanly before the longer hair continues beneath them.
- The side locks must be genuine cut sections, not temporary face-framing pieces or hair swept forward from the long length.
- When a fringe is present, make it a straight, dense front panel with a clean horizontal lower edge; do not turn it into curtain bangs.
- Keep the long back and side foundation visually continuous beneath the shorter side locks.
- The side-lock boundary must be crisp enough to read as intentional Hime architecture while retaining natural strand irregularity.
- Keep crown volume controlled and close to the skull.

### TEXTURE AND FINISH
Use predominantly straight, smooth hair with restrained natural fibre separation. Avoid heavy layering through the long foundation. The short side locks should retain enough density and bluntness to remain visibly distinct from ordinary face-framing layers.

### STYLE DIFFERENTIATION
The defining signal is the hard long-length foundation interrupted by two short blunt side locks, optionally paired with a blunt fringe. It must not become a jellyfish cut, long layered haircut, wolf cut, butterfly cut, shag or generic face-framing style.

## 6. NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS

No jellyfish cut, wolf cut, butterfly cut, shag, long layers, curtain bangs, wispy face framing, soft graduated side layers, bob, lob, mullet, pixie, heavily feathered ends, disconnected floating hair panels, exaggerated crown volume, artificial wig edge, face changes, ear regeneration, skin changes, lighting changes, background changes, zoom, crop or reframe.

The finished image must look like the same original photograph after a professional haircut, not a generated replacement person or synthetic wig.
