# Rachel Cut

## 1. INPAINT HAIR ONLY
Modify only the hair. Face, facial features, expression, eyes, brows, skin texture, skin tone, jawline, chin, neck, ears, body, clothing, background, lighting, exposure, color balance, focus, grain, pose, framing, perspective and source scale remain completely untouched.

## 2. GEOMETRY LOCK — ABSOLUTE
Preserve exact head position, tilt, rotation and viewing angle. Jaw, chin and neck are locked. Ear shape locked — do not regenerate the ears. The skull is a rigid fixed object. Only the surface hair changes. No zoom, crop, reframe or perspective change.

## 3. SKIN PRESERVATION
Preserve exact skin tone, undertone, pores, texture, expression and natural micro-variation. No smoothing, beautification, relighting, brightening or color drift. Never lighten the source skin tone.

## 4. HAIR COLOR — ROOT AUTHORITY
STEP 1 — Read true hair color ONLY from the first 2–3 mm at visible scalp roots, hairline, temples, part, crown and nape.
STEP 2 — Ignore background, lighting, clothing and environmental reflections.
STEP 3 — Reproduce exact root color with only natural source variation.
STEP 4 — White-background contamination check: the reproduced hair color must remain correct against pure white.

## 5. STYLE-SPECIFIC DESIGN
Create the recognizable 1990s-inspired Rachel Cut: a medium-length layered haircut with a voluminous crown, strong face-framing layers and a rounded, bouncy salon blowout silhouette. The haircut must read as structured layered movement rather than generic long layers.

### LENGTH / FOUNDATION
- Establish a medium-length foundation around shoulder to upper-chest level according to the source framing.
- Keep the overall perimeter below the shoulders but above the lower chest; do not turn it into a very long layered haircut.
- Retain enough perimeter weight to support the layered shape.

### FACE-FRAMING LAYERS
- Build prominent face-framing layers beginning around cheekbone-to-jaw level and progressively lengthening into the side lengths.
- The first face-framing layer must be clearly visible as a structural step, not a vague soft blend.
- Additional layers continue backward into the overall haircut so the framing remains integrated.
- Avoid curtain-bang architecture or a single disconnected panel.

### CROWN / INTERNAL LAYERS
- Create substantial internal layers through the crown and upper sides.
- Crown is visibly fuller than the lower lengths, with controlled rounded lift.
- Layer transitions are numerous enough to create the characteristic 1990s stacked movement, but not so fragmented that the style becomes a Shag or Wolf Cut.
- Keep the crown broad and rounded, not pointed or inflated vertically.

### SIDES / BACK
- Side lengths form a continuous graduated cascade from the short face-framing layers into longer shoulder-level lengths.
- Back carries multiple layers with a rounded, full silhouette rather than a single long sheet.
- Ends are softly flipped or curved by the blowout, not sharply curled or beach-waved.

### TEXTURE / FINISH
Use smooth salon-blowout movement with separated layered sections, natural bounce and controlled volume. Preserve believable strand variation and source hair texture. Avoid glossy plastic surfaces and repetitive identical curves.

### DIFFERENTIATION
Rachel Cut = medium-length layered foundation + prominent cheekbone/jaw face framing + rounded crown volume + 1990s blowout movement.
Distinct from Long Layers, which have a longer and less crown-heavy architecture; Butterfly Cut, which has more dramatic winged face framing; Shag, which is more fragmented and feathered; and Wolf Cut, which has stronger short-layer contrast.

## 6. NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No long-layer-only haircut, butterfly cut, wolf cut, modern shag, shag, hush cut, curtain bangs, blunt bob, lob, one-length hair, extreme 1980s feathering, giant crown, pageboy, wet-look hair, beach waves, tight curls, wig effect, face changes, skin changes, jaw reshaping, ear regeneration, head movement, lighting changes, background changes, zoom, crop or reframe.

The result must look like the same photograph after a professionally executed recognizable Rachel Cut.
