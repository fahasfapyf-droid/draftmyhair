# Blunt Bob

Create a true premium blunt bob defined by a dense, continuous blunt perimeter and substantial lower weight.

## PERIMETER GEOMETRY

- Build one strong, continuous lower perimeter around the intended jaw-to-chin bob length according to the source and target silhouette.
- The defining feature is an unbroken blunt edge with substantial visual density.
- Maintain consistent perimeter weight around the sides and back; do not create a wispy or heavily graduated outline.
- The blunt edge must look professionally cut while retaining microscopic natural strand irregularity.
- The haircut must remain a true bob rather than drifting into lob, pixie-bob, or layered-bob proportions.

## INTERNAL STRUCTURE

- Keep internal layering minimal.
- Preserve substantial lower weight beneath the surface.
- Avoid heavy graduation, feathering, shagging, or aggressive internal texturizing.
- The shape should come primarily from the perimeter and controlled weight distribution rather than visible layering.

## SURFACE AND VOLUME

- Maintain smooth, controlled volume that follows the fixed skull geometry.
- Hair should settle naturally from realistic roots into the dense perimeter.
- Individual fibres and small strand groups should remain photographically believable.
- Do not create an unnaturally perfect ruler edge; retain subtle real-hair irregularity without weakening the blunt structure.
- Do not introduce bangs or face-framing layers unless they already exist in the source.

## STYLE RECOGNITION

The result must immediately communicate a premium blunt bob: short bob length, strong continuous perimeter, dense lower weight, and minimal internal layering.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS

No layered bob, shaggy bob, feathering, heavy graduation, wispy perimeter, excessive texturing, lob, pixie-bob, exaggerated crown, or new bangs. Do not alter the face, jaw, chin, neck, ears, skin, lighting, background, framing, perspective, or source hair colour.
