# Textured Crop

## 1. INPAINT HAIR ONLY
Modify only the hair and the minimum hidden area required to reveal anatomy naturally covered by removed hair. Face, facial features, expression, eyes, brows, skin texture, skin tone, jawline, chin, neck, ears, body, clothing, background, lighting, exposure, color balance, focus, grain, pose, framing, perspective and source scale remain completely untouched.

## 2. GEOMETRY LOCK — ABSOLUTE
Preserve exact head position, tilt, rotation and viewing angle. Jaw, chin and neck are locked. Ear shape locked — do not regenerate the ears. The skull is a rigid fixed object. Only the surface hair changes. No zoom, crop, reframe, perspective change or head repositioning.

## 3. SKIN PRESERVATION
Preserve exact skin tone, undertone, pores, texture and expression. No smoothing, beautification, relighting, brightening, darkening or color drift. Never lighten the source skin tone.

## 4. HAIR COLOR — ROOT AUTHORITY
STEP 1 — Read true hair color ONLY from the first 2–3 mm at visible scalp roots, hairline, temples, part, crown and nape.
STEP 2 — Ignore background, lighting, clothing and environmental reflections.
STEP 3 — Reproduce exact root color with only natural source variation.
STEP 4 — White-background contamination check: reproduced hair color must remain correct against pure white.

## 5. STYLE-SPECIFIC DESIGN
Create a genuine Textured Crop: a short cropped haircut with compact sides/nape, controlled top length and deliberately visible dry texture. The crop silhouette must remain primary; texture is the surface system.

### CROP GEOMETRY
- Keep sides and nape short, tapered and close to the skull.
- Top/front is materially longer than sides but remains compact, approximately 1–2 inches depending on source scale.
- Lower perimeter stays cropped; nothing approaches bob or bixie length.
- Maintain a small, controlled head-hugging silhouette.

### TOP / FRONT
- Top carries the majority of visible length and texture.
- Front may form a short irregular crop fringe across the upper forehead, but it must not become a long fringe or French Bob.
- Crown remains low-to-moderate; no inflated crest.

### TEXTURE
- Use fine-to-medium irregular fibre groups, short piece separation and subtle directional variation.
- Texture is dry, tactile and broken rather than glossy or curled.
- Keep individual pieces connected at the root and naturally overlapping.
- Avoid identical spikes, repetitive clumps or artificial “textured hair” patterns.

### SIDES / NAPE
- Taper progressively around temples, ears and nape.
- Hair remains present unless the source architecture specifically calls for more exposure; do not introduce skin fades or shaved channels.
- Preserve natural hairline irregularity and ear anatomy.

### DIFFERENTIATION
Textured Crop = compact cropped geometry + short tapered sides/nape + longer textured top.
Distinct from French Crop by less dominant forward fringe, from Pixie by a more explicitly cropped/texture-led top, and from Bixie/Bob by materially shorter lower geometry.

## 6. NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No bob, bixie, lob, long crop, French bob, French crop with heavy fringe, pixie with long sides, undercut, skin fade, shaved channels, mohawk, faux hawk, pompadour, quiff, long fringe, curtain bangs, oversized crown, spiky uniform texture, wet look, plastic shine, crunchy product, wig effect, face changes, skin changes, jaw reshaping, ear regeneration, head movement, lighting changes, background changes, zoom, crop or reframe.

The result must look like the same photograph after a professionally executed Textured Crop.
