# Korean Layer Cut

## 1. INPAINT HAIR ONLY

Modify only the hair. The face, facial features, expression, eyes, brows, skin texture, skin tone, jawline, chin, neck, ears, body, clothing, background, lighting, exposure, color balance, focus, grain, pose, framing, perspective and source scale remain completely untouched.

## 2. GEOMETRY LOCK — ABSOLUTE

Preserve the exact head position, tilt and viewing angle. Any natural head tilt in the source is intentional and must remain unchanged. Jaw shape and width are locked. Chin is locked. Neck is locked. Ear shape locked — do not regenerate the ears. The skull is a rigid fixed object. Only the surface hair changes. Match source scale and framing exactly; no zoom, crop or reframe.

## 3. SKIN PRESERVATION — CRITICAL

Preserve exact facial and visible skin texture, pores, tone and natural micro-variation. No smoothing, beauty filter, retouching, relighting or skin-tone drift. Hair must not cast artificial color onto skin. Never lighten the source skin tone.

## 4. HAIR COLOR — ROOT AUTHORITY

STEP 1 — Read true hair color ONLY from the first 2–3 mm at the scalp, sampling hairline, temples, part and nape where visible.

STEP 2 — Ignore and discard color from background, lighting, environment, clothing and reflections.

STEP 3 — Reproduce the exact source root color and only natural variation already present. Do not invent highlights, lowlights or dye.

STEP 4 — White-background contamination check: would the reproduced hair color still look correct on a pure white background? If no, correct the contamination.

## 5. STYLE-SPECIFIC DESIGN

Create a true modern Korean Layer Cut defined by a long, polished layered foundation, controlled crown, soft diagonal face framing and deliberate internal movement concentrated around the front and mid-lengths.

### LAYER ARCHITECTURE
- Preserve a medium-to-long overall foundation rather than shortening the haircut into a bob or shag.
- Begin the most visible face-framing layer around the cheekbone-to-jaw region, then lengthen progressively into the mid-lengths.
- Add a second, longer internal layer through the upper-to-mid side sections so the front layers merge continuously into the main length.
- Keep the crown relatively smooth and low-volume; the movement should increase below the crown rather than forming a high layered cap.
- Maintain a clean, elongated external silhouette with controlled width.

### FACE FRAMING
Use soft, diagonal framing that follows the cheek and jaw before flowing backward into longer layers. It must not form curtain bangs or a short disconnected fringe.

### TEXTURE AND FINISH
Use smooth salon-polished strand grouping with soft inward/outward movement at the ends. Layer transitions should be visible but low-contrast and continuous. Avoid aggressive choppiness.

### DIFFERENTIATION
The defining combination is long polished foundation + multiple integrated front/mid layers + restrained crown + soft diagonal face framing. Keep it distinct from Hush Cut, Butterfly Cut, Shag, Wolf Cut, Curtain Layers and generic Long Layers.

## 6. NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS

No hush cut, butterfly cut, wolf cut, shag, mullet, heavy feathering, curtain bangs, curtain layers, short disconnected face-framing panels, blunt bob, lob, bixie, pixie, high crown volume, choppy texture, exaggerated wings, generic one-layer long haircut, wig effect, face changes, ear regeneration, skin changes, lighting changes, background changes, zoom, crop or reframe.

The finished image must look like the same original photograph after a professional haircut, not a regenerated person or synthetic wig.
