# Textured Bob

## 1. INPAINT HAIR ONLY
Modify only the hair and the minimum hidden area required to reveal anatomy naturally covered by removed hair. Face, facial features, expression, eyes, brows, skin texture, skin tone, jawline, chin, neck, ears, body, clothing, background, lighting, exposure, color balance, focus, grain, pose, framing, perspective and source scale remain completely untouched.

## 2. GEOMETRY LOCK — ABSOLUTE
Preserve exact head position, tilt, rotation and viewing angle. Jaw, chin and neck are locked. Ear shape locked — do not regenerate the ears. The skull is a rigid fixed object. Only the surface hair changes. No zoom, crop, reframe, perspective change or head repositioning.

## 3. SKIN PRESERVATION
Preserve exact skin tone, undertone, pores, texture and expression. No smoothing, beautification, relighting, brightening, darkening or color drift. Never lighten the source skin tone.

## 4. HAIR COLOR — ROOT AUTHORITY
STEP 1 — Read true hair color ONLY from the first 2–3 mm at visible scalp roots, hairline, temples, part, crown and nape.
STEP 2 — Ignore background, lighting, clothing and environmental reflections.
STEP 3 — Reproduce exact root color with only natural source variation.
STEP 4 — White-background contamination check: reproduced hair color must remain correct against pure white.

## 5. STYLE-SPECIFIC DESIGN
Create a true Textured Bob: a recognizable short-to-medium bob silhouette whose primary distinction is controlled surface texture and piece separation, while its underlying perimeter remains coherent and bob-like. Texture must not become shag architecture.

### LENGTH / FOUNDATION
- Keep the dominant foundation around chin to upper-neck/short-bob territory, clearly shorter than a lob.
- Preserve a readable overall bob perimeter with moderate lower weight.
- Nape remains compact; no long tail or shoulder-length extension.

### STRUCTURE
- Use restrained internal weight release rather than numerous short layers.
- Texture is distributed through the surface and ends, not built as a stack of visible layer shelves.
- Maintain a calm crown and coherent side shape.

### TEXTURE SYSTEM
- Create fine-to-medium strand grouping with irregular piece separation, subtle feathering and natural dry movement.
- Texture should be visible primarily through broken highlight/shadow grouping and slightly varied strand exits.
- Keep most major sections connected; do not create disconnected shag panels.
- Ends can be lightly choppy but must retain enough density to preserve the bob silhouette.

### FACE / FRONT
- Use subtle integrated front movement from cheekbone through jaw only where needed.
- No dominant curtain panel, butterfly wing or long wolf-style framing.
- Preserve the underlying parting and natural front direction.

### FINISH
Touchably textured, matte-to-natural, lived-in and salon-realistic. No beach-wave pattern, wet gloss, crunchy product, artificial flyaway explosion or repeated digital strand pattern.

### DIFFERENTIATION
Textured Bob = coherent bob geometry + moderate perimeter weight + controlled surface texture.
Distinct from Shaggy Bob, which has stronger internal layer fragmentation; Layered Bob, which relies more on visible structural layers; Blunt/Structured Blunt Bob, which emphasize a clean edge; and Textured Crop, which is materially shorter and more cropped.

## 6. NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No shaggy bob, shag, modern shag, wolf cut, butterfly cut, mullet, lob, long bob, pixie, bixie, blunt one-length bob, heavy graduation, disconnected layers, long face-framing wings, curtain bangs, excessive crown volume, chunky texture, beach waves, wet look, greasy hair, plastic shine, crunchy styling, wig effect, face changes, skin changes, jaw reshaping, ear regeneration, head movement, lighting changes, background changes, zoom, crop or reframe.

The result must look like the same photograph after a professionally executed Textured Bob.
