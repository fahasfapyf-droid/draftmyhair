# C-Cut

Create a true C-Cut defined by a broad, continuous C-shaped length profile across the back and sides.

## C-SHAPED LENGTH GEOMETRY
- The center-back section must be shorter than the outer back sections, while the side lengths gradually become longer toward the front.
- Build one broad shallow C profile from the rear, not a straight baseline and not a deep V.
- The transition from center to sides must be gradual and continuous; avoid abrupt steps.
- Keep the longest visible side sections clearly longer than the center-back section.
- The curvature must remain broad and shallow enough to distinguish a C-Cut from a U-Cut.

## INTERNAL STRUCTURE
- Use subtle long internal layering only where required to support the C-shaped perimeter.
- Preserve enough lower weight for the curved perimeter to remain visually continuous.
- The shape must come primarily from the perimeter length relationship, not from styling.
- Keep face framing subordinate to the C-shaped perimeter unless framing already exists.

## TEXTURE AND FLOW
- Maintain natural hair behaviour appropriate to the source texture.
- Use realistic strand grouping and subtle independent fibre variation.
- Ends should settle naturally and preserve the intended curvature without forming artificial flips.
- No exaggerated volume or salon-blowout effect.

## STYLE RECOGNITION
The result must immediately read as a C-Cut through its broad shallow C profile: shorter center-back, progressively longer sides, smooth transition, and no sharp V point.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No U-Cut, V-Cut, straight blunt hemline, layered shag, butterfly cut, dramatic flipped ends, or artificial curve painted onto the hair. Do not alter the face, jaw, chin, neck, ears, skin, lighting, background, framing, perspective, or source hair colour.
