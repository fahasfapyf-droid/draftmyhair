# Chelsea Cut

Create a true Chelsea Cut defined by a compact, close-fitting short crown and upper-side structure contrasted against substantially longer hair retained through the back.

## PRIMARY ARCHITECTURE

- Keep the crown and upper sides visibly short, close to the skull, and compact.
- Preserve substantially longer hair through the back so the front/top-to-back length contrast is unmistakable.
- The long back must remain a continuous, substantial section rather than appearing as extensions or isolated tails.
- The short upper zone must remain shorter than the back even when the hair settles naturally.
- Do not allow the crown to become a conventional pixie, mullet, shag, or faux-hawk silhouette.

## TRANSITION

- Build the short-to-long relationship through actual haircut geometry, not by sweeping or pinning the long back away from the short area.
- The transition should be clear enough to communicate the Chelsea architecture while remaining naturally integrated.
- Avoid a hard digital boundary, fade, or undercut treatment.
- Preserve believable overlap and occlusion where the short upper hair meets the longer rear mass.

## BACK LENGTH AND WEIGHT

- Retain enough length and density in the back for the defining contrast to remain visible.
- Let the long back fall naturally under gravity with realistic weight and directional flow.
- The back should remain visually longer than the crown and upper sides from the source viewing angle.
- Do not shorten the back into a generic short haircut.

## SURFACE AND TEXTURE

- Keep the short crown controlled with natural strand grouping and restrained volume.
- Preserve realistic fibre separation and microscopic end irregularity.
- Maintain natural root density and believable scalp integration.
- Do not introduce bangs or dramatic side-sweeping unless already present.

## STYLE RECOGNITION

The result must immediately read as a Chelsea Cut through one dominant visual relationship: a very short, close-fitting upper/crown region contrasted with a clearly longer, continuous back section.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS

No ordinary pixie, mullet, shag, long pixie, bob, faux hawk, mohawk, fade, disconnected undercut, generic short-over-long haircut, or artificial wig effect. Do not alter the face, jaw, chin, neck, ears, skin, lighting, background, framing, perspective, or source hair colour.
