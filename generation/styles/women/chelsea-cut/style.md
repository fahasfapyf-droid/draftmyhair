# Chelsea Cut

Create a true Chelsea Cut defined by a very short, close-fitting crown and side structure contrasted against substantially longer hair retained at the back.

## CHELSEA ARCHITECTURE
- Keep the crown and upper sides visibly short and close to the head.
- Maintain a clear transition from the short upper section into the longer back section.
- Preserve substantially longer hair through the back so the front/top-to-back length contrast is immediately visible.
- The short upper zone must remain compact and controlled rather than becoming a generic pixie or crop.
- The long back must remain a coherent continuous section rather than isolated extensions.

## TRANSITION AND WEIGHT
- Build the short-to-long relationship through deliberate haircut geometry, not by styling the long hair away from the short section.
- Keep the transition visually clear but naturally integrated, with no artificial hard digital boundary.
- Retain enough density in the long back to communicate its defining contrast with the short crown.
- Keep the short crown low and close to the skull; do not inflate it.

## TEXTURE AND FLOW
- Allow the long back to fall naturally under gravity with realistic weight and directional flow.
- Keep the short crown controlled with natural strand grouping and subtle fibre variation.
- Preserve realistic overlap where short upper hair meets the longer rear section.
- Do not add bangs or dramatic side-sweeping unless already present.

## STYLE RECOGNITION
The result must immediately read as a Chelsea Cut: short close-fitting crown and upper sides paired with a distinctly longer back. The front-to-back contrast is the defining architectural feature.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No ordinary pixie, mullet, shag, long pixie, bob, faux hawk, mohawk, fade, disconnected undercut, or generic short-over-long haircut. Do not alter the face, jaw, chin, neck, ears, skin, lighting, background, framing, perspective, or source hair colour.
