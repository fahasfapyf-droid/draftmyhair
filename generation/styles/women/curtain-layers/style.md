# Curtain Layers

Create a true Curtain Layers haircut: a layered haircut whose primary face-framing architecture opens away from the center part like soft curtains, while the framing remains integrated into longer surrounding layers rather than becoming a separate bang block.

## FACE-FRAMING ARCHITECTURE
- Establish two balanced front framing sections that begin around the cheekbone-to-jaw region and lengthen progressively into the surrounding haircut.
- The framing should open away from the center naturally, creating a curtain-like visual flow without a solid blunt fringe.
- Keep the shortest framing point clearly longer than a conventional eyebrow-level curtain bang unless the source already contains a shorter fringe.
- Integrate both framing sections into the next-longer layers so there is no abrupt shelf or disconnected panel.

## INTERNAL LAYERING
- Use soft, continuous layers through the mid-lengths and front to support the curtain movement.
- Keep the crown relatively calm; do not create butterfly-cut crown lift or shaggy choppiness.
- Preserve a longer overall silhouette with gradual length distribution rather than stacked layers.

## TEXTURE AND FLOW
Allow natural separation around the face, realistic strand grouping, restrained movement, and microscopic end variation. The haircut should communicate curtain-like face framing through geometry and layering, not through dramatic styling.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No solid curtain fringe, heavy bangs, blunt bang wall, butterfly crown, shag, wolf cut, face-framing-only haircut, disconnected panels, aggressive step layers, or exaggerated blowout. Do not alter the face, jaw, chin, neck, ears, skin, lighting, background, framing, perspective, or source hair colour.
