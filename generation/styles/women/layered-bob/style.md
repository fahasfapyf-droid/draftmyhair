# Layered Bob

Create a true Layered Bob as a compact bob-length haircut whose identity comes from clearly visible internal layering while retaining a coherent bob perimeter.

## LENGTH / PERIMETER
- Keep overall length around chin to upper-neck level, clearly shorter than a lob.
- Maintain a deliberate lower perimeter with moderate weight.
- The edge may be softened by layering, but the silhouette must still read immediately as a bob.
- Avoid a thin transparent perimeter.

## INTERNAL LAYER ARCHITECTURE
- Create several controlled internal layers through the crown, upper sides and lower side sections.
- Establish a clear hierarchy: shorter upper structure, intermediate side layers, longer bob perimeter.
- Layering must be visibly stronger than Classic, Blunt and Structured Blunt Bob.
- Keep transitions integrated rather than creating short choppy shelves.
- Preserve enough lower weight that the haircut remains a bob rather than becoming a shag.

## SILHOUETTE / CROWN
Keep the crown controlled and the overall silhouette compact. Internal layers create visible movement and separation, but do not produce a high crown or broad shag silhouette.

## TEXTURE / FINISH
Use realistic strand grouping, natural weight transitions and subtle inward/outward variation between layer levels. The layering should be readable through actual length differences and shadow/overlap, not through exaggerated styling.

## STYLE DIFFERENTIATION
Layered Bob = bob length + recognizable perimeter + multiple visible internal layers.
It is more structurally layered than Classic/Blunt/Structured Blunt Bob, cleaner than Shaggy Bob, less diffused than Soft Layered Bob, and more compact than long-layered styles.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No classic one-length bob, blunt bob, structured blunt bob, French bob, Italian bob, soft lob, shaggy bob, shag, wolf cut, butterfly cut, pixie, bixie, long layered haircut, disconnected shelves, excessive crown volume, heavy choppy fragmentation or wig effect.
