STYLE TARGET
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Create genuine modern Long Layers.

The defining characteristics are:

• preserved long overall length
• cascading internal layers
• chin-to-collarbone starting point
• soft face framing
• retained fullness
• feathered/tapered ends
• natural movement
• realistic strand irregularity

The hairstyle must immediately read as long layered hair.

It must NOT read as:

• medium layers
• long bob
• lob
• shag
• wolf cut
• butterfly cut
• heavily feathered cut
• blunt long hair

This is a structural layering transformation, not a shortening
transformation.

The photograph must never adapt to the hairstyle.

Only the surface hair changes.

HAIR COLOR — ROOT AUTHORITY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Determine intrinsic hair colour exclusively from the original root zone.

Read approximately the first 2–3 mm emerging from the scalp across
visible:

• frontal hairline
• temples
• part
• crown

Ignore environmental colour influence from:

• lighting
• clothing
• background
• reflected surroundings

Reproduce the exact intrinsic source colour throughout the hairstyle.

Preserve only natural tonal variation already present.

Do not:

• invent highlights
• increase saturation
• warm the colour
• cool the colour
• change the colour family
• introduce artificial gloss
• introduce artificial colour gradients

Self-check:

If the hair were viewed against a neutral white background, the
intrinsic colour should remain correct and uncontaminated.

LENGTH PRESERVATION — PRIMARY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Maintain long overall length.

Hair must extend well below the shoulders.

Perceived final length should remain approximately the same as, or
slightly longer than, the source where the source already contains
long hair.

Do NOT shorten the haircut into:

• short
• medium
• bob
• lob
• shoulder-length

This is a layering transformation.

The existing long-hair character must remain unmistakable.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
LAYER ARCHITECTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Create soft cascading layers.

Layering begins approximately around:

• chin
through
• collarbone

The exact starting position may vary naturally with the source
geometry and existing hairline.

Layers must cascade progressively through the long lengths.

Avoid abrupt layer transitions.

Avoid obvious step cuts.

Avoid symmetrical layer placement.

Layer structure should emerge through natural weight distribution.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FRONT / FACE FRAMING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Create subtle face-framing layers beginning near chin level.

The front layers naturally taper toward the longer lengths.

Maintain an open forehead.

No:

• heavy bangs
• curtain fringe
• dramatic side fringe
• short face-framing chunks

Face framing should remain understated and integrated with the main
layer structure.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
MID-LENGTH STRUCTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Introduce internal layering through the mid-lengths.

The purpose is to:

• remove excess bulk
• create natural movement
• preserve fullness
• prevent a single-sheet appearance

The hair must not become excessively thin.

Maintain substantial long-hair density.

Avoid uniform layer sections.

Allow natural variation in strand grouping and weight.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
ENDS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Create lightly feathered and softly tapered ends.

Ends must retain individual strand definition.

Do not blur or excessively soften the perimeter.

Allow slight variation in taper density.

Some sections may remain marginally fuller than others.

Avoid:

• blunt ends
• razor-perfect ends
• excessive feathering
• wispy disappearance
• artificial taper patterns

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
TEXTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Preserve the subject's natural hair texture.

If the source is:

• straight → preserve straight character
• naturally wavy → preserve wave character
• naturally textured → preserve its intrinsic texture

Do not impose an unrelated curl pattern.

Introduce realistic high-frequency strand variation:

• subtle micro-contrast between adjacent strands
• irregular strand thickness
• irregular strand spacing
• localized density variation
• natural strand grouping

Avoid flat tonal regions.

Avoid synthetic smoothness.

Introduce controlled natural variation:

• slight uneven strand separation
• fine flyaways
• broken strand grouping
• localized overlap
• subtle density variation
• small differences in strand direction

Variation must remain controlled.

Do not create:

• frizz
• disorder
• excessive flyaways
• editorial texture
• artificial randomness

Hair follows natural gravity and the source's existing growth direction.

Avoid:

• mirrored strand flow
• repeated curvature
• identical strand trajectories
• mechanically uniform layers

Each major section should settle according to:

• length
• weight
• gravity
• source pose
• neighbouring hair

Movement should appear incidental and photographic.

Allow slight natural lift at the crown.

The lift must remain realistic.

No blowout.

No inflated crown.

No excessive fullness.

Volume must remain consistent with the source hair density.

The crown must follow the original skull geometry.

STYLE DISTINCTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

LONG LAYERS:

→ long overall length
→ cascading layers
→ chin-to-collarbone starting point
→ preserved fullness
→ soft face framing
→ tapered ends

BUTTERFLY CUT:

→ stronger upper-layer separation
→ more dramatic face framing
→ greater volume contrast

SHAG:

→ more aggressive texture
→ shorter disconnected layers
→ stronger perimeter breakup

WOLF CUT:

→ stronger crown-to-length contrast
→ more aggressive layering
→ more pronounced shag structure

Long Layers must remain comparatively soft,
continuous, and wearable.

SELF-CORRECTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

If the result reads as medium-length:

→ restore the long overall length.

If the result reads as a Lob:

→ extend the lower lengths well beyond the shoulders.

If the result reads as a Butterfly Cut:

→ reduce upper-layer separation and dramatic face framing.

If the result reads as a Shag:

→ reduce texture and disconnected layer structure.

If the result reads as a Wolf Cut:

→ reduce crown contrast and aggressive layering.

If the result reads as blunt long hair:

→ increase internal layering and tapered movement.
