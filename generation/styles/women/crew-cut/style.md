# Crew Cut

Create a true Crew Cut defined by a short tapered side/back structure supporting a longer graduated top, with the front-top section slightly longer than the rear top so the classic forward-directed profile remains visible.

## TOP GRADUATION

- Keep the top clearly longer than the sides and back.
- Graduate the top from slightly longer at the front hairline toward shorter at the rear crown rather than making the entire top one uniform length.
- Keep the top controlled and close enough to the skull to read as a haircut, not a quiff or pompadour.
- Let the front-top hair settle naturally forward or slightly upward according to the source hair's behaviour.

## SIDES AND BACK

- Taper the sides and nape progressively shorter while retaining believable hair coverage.
- Keep the taper clean and compact but do not create a skin fade, hard disconnect, or undercut.
- Preserve natural sideburn and nape transitions and realistic density around the ears.

## SURFACE AND RECOGNITION

Use restrained texture, realistic short-hair fibre separation, natural root density, and subtle length variation. The defining hierarchy is longer graduated top + progressively shorter sides/back + compact tapered silhouette.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS

No buzz cut, fade, skin fade, undercut, disconnected crop, Caesar crop, pompadour, quiff, mohawk, bowl cut, long top, exaggerated spikes, or artificial clipper-painted surface. Do not alter the face, jaw, chin, neck, ears, skin, lighting, background, framing, perspective, or source hair colour.
