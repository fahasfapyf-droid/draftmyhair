# Bixie

Create a true Bixie: a compact hybrid between a short bob and a pixie, with a visibly bob-connected upper/perimeter structure and a shorter controlled nape.

## BIXIE ARCHITECTURE

- Keep the nape and lower sides distinctly shorter than the upper sides and crown.
- Retain more length and visible weight through the upper sides, cheek-to-jaw region, and crown than in a conventional pixie.
- The upper hair must remain connected to a compact bob-like perimeter rather than collapsing into a pure pixie silhouette.
- The shorter nape must be clearly intentional but smoothly integrated into the overall haircut.
- Maintain a compact head-hugging silhouette; do not create a grown-out pixie or full bob.

## WEIGHT DISTRIBUTION

- Preserve visible upper-side weight that distinguishes the Bixie from a conventional pixie.
- Keep the nape controlled and lighter without producing a disconnected undercut.
- Crown volume should be restrained and structurally supported by the haircut rather than inflated styling.
- The overall silhouette should remain compact, with no bob-length nape.

## TEXTURE AND FLOW

- Use natural directional flow through the crown and upper sides.
- Create realistic strand grouping with fine separation and subtle fibre variation.
- Ends should show controlled salon cutting with microscopic natural irregularity rather than a hard CGI edge.
- Do not introduce dramatic bangs unless bangs already exist in the source.

## STYLE RECOGNITION

The result must immediately read as a Bixie: visibly shorter and more compact than a bob, but with more upper-side length and bob-connected weight than a pure pixie.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS

No pure pixie, full bob, grown-out pixie, generic short haircut, helmet shape, excessive crown height, bob-length nape, disconnected undercut, or artificial wig effect. Do not alter the face, jaw, chin, neck, ears, skin, lighting, background, framing, perspective, or source hair colour.
