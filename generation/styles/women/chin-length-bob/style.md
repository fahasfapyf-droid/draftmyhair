# Chin-Length Bob

Create a true chin-length bob defined by a controlled perimeter that terminates at the chin region.

## LENGTH AND PERIMETER
- The primary lower perimeter must terminate at the chin region.
- Maintain a continuous bob perimeter around the sides and back with the chin as the defining length reference.
- Keep the silhouette compact and proportionate to the fixed head geometry.
- Do not allow the cut to drift into a shorter micro bob or a longer lob silhouette.

## INTERNAL STRUCTURE
- Keep internal layering restrained so the chin-length perimeter remains the dominant structural signal.
- Preserve moderate lower weight and a clean continuous outline.
- Avoid heavy graduation, shagging, feathering, or excessive texturizing.
- Face-framing pieces remain subordinate to the chin-length perimeter unless already present.

## SURFACE AND VOLUME
- Maintain controlled natural volume that follows the fixed skull.
- Hair should fall naturally into the chin-level perimeter with realistic root density and directional flow.
- Use believable strand grouping and microscopic natural variation at the cut edge.
- Do not create a ruler-perfect perimeter.

## STYLE RECOGNITION
The result must immediately read as a premium chin-length bob. The defining feature is precise chin-region termination combined with a compact continuous bob silhouette.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No micro bob, jaw-length bob, blunt lob, long bob, shaggy bob, heavily layered bob, exaggerated graduation, or artificial wig effect. Do not alter the face, jaw, chin, neck, ears, skin, lighting, background, framing, perspective, or source hair colour.
