# Faux Hawk

Create a true Faux Hawk defined by a concentrated central strip of longer hair that rises or gathers above substantially shorter side sections, while the sides remain hair-covered rather than shaved into a Mohawk.

## CENTRAL STRIP GEOMETRY

- Preserve a continuous central strip from the frontal top through the crown toward the rear top/nape direction.
- The central strip must be visibly longer and fuller than both side zones.
- Keep the strip narrower than the full top width so the side-to-center contrast is immediately readable.
- Allow the central hair to project modestly upward and toward the center, creating a controlled ridge rather than a tall theatrical crest.
- Maintain enough length at the front and crown to establish a connected central structure.

## SIDE ZONES

- Keep both sides distinctly shorter than the central strip but still visibly covered with natural hair.
- Use progressive shortening toward the sides without creating a skin fade, shaved channel, or hard disconnected undercut.
- The transition from short side zones into the central strip must be intentional and continuous.

## STYLE DIFFERENTIATION

A Faux Hawk differs from a Mohawk because the side zones remain hair-covered and the central ridge is integrated into the surrounding haircut rather than isolated by shaved or near-shaved channels. It must not collapse into a generic textured crop.

## SURFACE AND FINISH

Use restrained directional texture and realistic short-hair fibre separation. The central ridge should have natural strand grouping and gravity-aware lift, not gelled spikes or plastic uniformity.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS

No Mohawk, shaved-side Mohawk, skin fade, undercut, disconnected crop, quiff, pompadour, high pompadour, spiked punk crest, bowl cut, or generic textured crop. Do not alter the face, jaw, chin, neck, ears, skin, lighting, background, framing, perspective, or source hair colour.
