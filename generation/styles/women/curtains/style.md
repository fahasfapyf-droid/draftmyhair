# Curtains

Create true Curtain Bangs / Curtains as the defining hairstyle feature: a deliberate center-parted fringe that separates into two visible panels and frames the forehead and upper face while remaining naturally integrated into the surrounding hair.

## FRINGE ARCHITECTURE
- Establish a clear central part in the frontal hairline and divide the fringe into two left/right sections.
- Each section should be shortest near the center part and progressively lengthen toward the outer temple and cheek area, creating the characteristic open-curtain shape.
- The inner edges must part cleanly without becoming a solid straight-across fringe.
- Keep the curtain panels substantial enough to read as intentional bangs, but soft enough to retain natural strand variation.
- Integrate the outer ends into the adjacent side hair so the fringe does not look clipped on or disconnected.

## STYLING AND SURFACE
- Keep the curtain opening visible at rest; do not flatten the panels into ordinary bangs.
- Allow restrained outward sweep and natural fall, with no exaggerated retro volume.
- Preserve realistic root emergence at the center part, natural density, fine fibre separation, and microscopic edge variation.

## STYLE DIFFERENTIATION
This style is defined by the actual center-parted fringe architecture. The rest of the haircut may remain relatively simple; do not invent a separate layered haircut merely to communicate Curtains.

## NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No blunt full fringe, side fringe, heavy straight bangs, curtain layers without bangs, dramatic 1970s blowout, bouffant, shag, wolf cut, or disconnected bang panels. Do not alter the face, forehead, brows, eyes, jaw, chin, neck, ears, skin, lighting, background, framing, perspective, or source hair colour.
