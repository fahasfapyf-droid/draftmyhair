# Soft Layered French Bob

## 1. INPAINT HAIR ONLY
Modify only the hair and the minimum hidden area required to reveal anatomy naturally covered by removed hair. Face, facial features, expression, eyes, brows, skin texture, skin tone, jawline, chin, neck, ears, body, clothing, background, lighting, exposure, color balance, focus, grain, pose, framing, perspective and source scale remain completely untouched.

## 2. GEOMETRY LOCK — ABSOLUTE
Preserve exact head position, tilt, rotation and viewing angle. Jaw, chin and neck are locked. Ear shape locked — do not regenerate the ears. The skull is a rigid fixed object. Only the surface hair changes. No zoom, crop, reframe, perspective change or head repositioning.

## 3. SKIN PRESERVATION
Preserve exact skin tone, undertone, pores, texture and expression. No smoothing, beautification, relighting, brightening, darkening or color drift. Never lighten the source skin tone.

## 4. HAIR COLOR — ROOT AUTHORITY
STEP 1 — Read true hair color ONLY from the first 2–3 mm at visible scalp roots, hairline, temples, part, crown and nape.
STEP 2 — Ignore background, lighting, clothing and environmental reflections.
STEP 3 — Reproduce exact root color with only natural source variation.
STEP 4 — White-background contamination check: reproduced hair color must remain correct against pure white.

## 5. STYLE-SPECIFIC DESIGN
Create a genuine Soft Layered French Bob: compact chin-level Parisian bob geometry with subtle internal layers, light density, a softly diffused perimeter and understated natural texture. The French identity comes from compact proportion, airy weight and effortless texture—not from generic “soft” wording.

### FRENCH BOB GEOMETRY
- Keep the dominant perimeter at or slightly above chin level.
- Compact side and back proportions; no lob extension.
- Silhouette is lightly rounded and close to the skull, never boxy, triangular or helmet-like.
- Maintain a lightweight but coherent bob perimeter.

### INTERNAL LAYERS
- Use restrained internal layers through crown and upper sides to release weight.
- Layer transitions remain short, blended and mostly visible through movement rather than hard steps.
- Do not create shag, wolf or heavily graduated architecture.

### FRENCH TEXTURE / FINISH
- Create soft natural bend, slight separation and airy lived-in texture.
- Ends remain lightly diffused and imperfect rather than ruler-sharp.
- Finish should feel naturally air-dried/softly styled, not heavily blown out or glossy.
- Preserve fine strand variation and realistic fibre overlap.

### FRONT / FRAMING
- Open, breathable front architecture; no mandatory heavy fringe.
- If a fringe exists in the source/style interpretation, keep it soft, irregular and integrated rather than blunt or curtain-like.
- Face-framing pieces remain short and compact within the French bob geometry.

### DIFFERENTIATION
Soft Layered French Bob = compact chin-level French Bob + restrained internal layering + airy diffused perimeter + understated Parisian texture.
Distinct from French Bob No Bangs/With Bangs by its softer internal movement; Italian Bob by lighter density and less rounded blowout; Soft Layered Bob by shorter compact French proportions and more airy texture; Structured Blunt Bob by its diffused perimeter.

## 6. NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No Italian bob, structured blunt bob, blunt bob, soft lob, long bob, bixie, pixie, shaggy bob, shag, wolf cut, heavy graduation, long face framing, butterfly wings, heavy blunt fringe, curtain bangs, helmet shape, mushroom volume, large blowout, wet look, plastic shine, excessive polish, wig effect, face changes, skin changes, jaw reshaping, ear regeneration, head movement, lighting changes, background changes, zoom, crop or reframe.

The result must look like the same photograph after a professionally executed Soft Layered French Bob.
